# Cash-flow forecast experiment

Generated by `python experiments/forecast/run.py`. Synthetic data only.

- 150 synthetic businesses (seeds 5000–5149), 24 months each, monthly **net business inflow** computed by the real evidence rules and metrics code.
- Rolling origin: origins at months 14–20, horizons 1–3; at each origin LightGBM is retrained on targets already observed. Runs tracked in MLflow (`experiments/forecast/mlflow.db`).

| Model | MAE h=1 | MAE h=2 | MAE h=3 | MAPE h=1 | MAPE h=2 | MAPE h=3 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Last value | ₹30,994 | ₹28,254 | ₹36,669 | 14.1% | 13.0% | 14.8% |
| Seasonal naive (same month last year) | ₹30,235 | ₹29,656 | ₹28,559 | 14.2% | 13.2% | 11.8% |
| LightGBM (lags 1-3, 12; month; type) | ₹24,843 | ₹23,245 | ₹28,491 | 11.5% | 10.8% | 12.2% |

**Decision:** LightGBM beats seasonal naive on MAE at every horizon (h=1: 17.8%, h=2: 21.6%, h=3: 0.2% lower). That is necessary but not sufficient: the model was trained and tested on synthetic businesses, so **the app shows no forecast** until this experiment is repeated on real statement histories. A margin of a fraction of a percent (if any horizon shows one) is within noise.

## What this does and doesn't show

- The generator's seasonality is simple (weekend +20%, October–November +35%) and the same every year, which favours seasonal naive. Real businesses have trends, shocks and holidays that move.
- 24 months is the minimum for a 12-month lag; most real borrowers won't have that much statement history, which limits any forecast in practice.
- MAPE is unstable when a month's net inflow is near zero; MAE (in rupees) is the primary metric.
